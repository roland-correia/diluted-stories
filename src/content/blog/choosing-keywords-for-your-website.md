Most businesses pick their website keywords by guessing. They use the words they use inside the business, which are often not the words customers type into a search box.

Here is a simple way to do it properly.

## Start with what customers ask

Write down the questions and phrases customers use when they first contact you. Emails, calls, reviews and social media comments are all good sources. A plumber might say "boiler service", but customers might write "boiler making a noise" or "how often should I service my boiler".

Those phrases are your starting list.

## Understand what the searcher wants

Every search has a reason behind it, and it usually falls into one of four groups:

- **Learning:** "how does a heat pump work"
- **Comparing:** "best accounting software for small business"
- **Buying:** "book a boiler service near me"
- **Finding a specific site:** "your business name"

A page that explains something will not rank well for someone who wants to buy, and the other way round. Decide which group each phrase belongs to before you decide where to use it.

## Prefer specific phrases

Short, broad searches like "marketing" are very competitive and often vague. Longer phrases like "marketing for small restaurants" have fewer searches, but the people searching know what they want. For a smaller business those are usually the better ones to aim at first.

## Check what is already working

Google Search Console is free. Once your site is connected, it shows the searches people already use to find you, how often your pages appeared, and how often they were clicked. Pages that appear a lot but get few clicks usually need a clearer title or description.

You can also search your main phrases yourself and look at who ranks. If every result is a big directory, that phrase is probably not worth chasing. If you see businesses like yours, it is realistic.

## One main phrase per page

Give each page one main keyword and write for a person first. Put the phrase in the page title, the main heading and the opening paragraph, then write the rest naturally. Repeating it over and over does not help and makes the page worse to read.

If two pages are trying to rank for the same phrase, they compete with each other. Merge them, or change one to target something different.

## Keep checking

Search habits change, and so does the competition. Look at your Search Console results every month or two, notice what is growing, and update or add pages to match.
