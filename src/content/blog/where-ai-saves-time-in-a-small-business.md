AI tools can take a lot of repetitive work off a small team. They can also produce confident nonsense. The skill is knowing which jobs suit them.

## Jobs that suit AI

**First drafts.** Emails, product descriptions, social posts and quotes can all be drafted in seconds. You still edit them, but starting from a draft is faster than starting from a blank page.

**Summaries.** Long email threads, meeting notes and customer feedback can be boiled down to the key points and next steps.

**Sorting and tagging.** Incoming enquiries can be grouped by type, so the urgent ones get seen first. The same goes for reviews and survey answers.

**Routine replies.** For questions you answer every week, such as opening hours, delivery times and how to book, AI can suggest replies that you approve or tweak.

**Spreadsheet help.** Cleaning up lists, writing formulas and explaining what a report is showing are all things AI does well.

## Jobs that need a person

**Anything with facts you have not checked.** AI can state wrong prices, dates and policies as if they were certain. Check every figure and claim before it goes out under your name.

**Sensitive conversations.** A complaint or a difficult message needs someone who understands the customer and your business.

**Final decisions.** Use AI to prepare options. Make the choice yourself.

## Protect your customers' data

Before you paste anything into an AI tool, ask whether it contains personal information about customers or staff. Check what the tool does with what you enter, and whether it uses it to train its models. If you are not sure, leave the personal details out. In the UK, data protection law applies to how you handle that information.

## Start small

Pick one task your team repeats every week and does not enjoy. Try an AI tool on that, check the results carefully for a few weeks, and keep it only if it saves time without lowering quality. Then move on to the next task.
